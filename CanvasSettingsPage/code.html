Core Idea: The circulation of a vector field F around a closed boundary curve ∂S equals the net flux of curl(F) passing through the surface S!
Exam 2 Focus ★
Fundamental Formula of Stokes' Theorem
∮_{∂S} F · dr = ∬_S (∇ × F) · dS = ∬_S (∇ × F) · n̂ dσ
Boundary orientation induced via Right-Hand Rule: fingers wrap along curve ∂S, thumb points along unit normal n̂ (r'(t) × n̂ > 0)
Green's Theorem Reduction

When S is flat in xy-plane with n̂ = k̂, Stokes reduces to 2D planar curl scalar:
∮_C (P dx + Q dy) = ∬_R (∂Q/∂x - ∂P/∂y) dA

1. Smooth Surface: S is piecewise-smooth oriented 2D manifold bounded by closed loop ∂S.
2. Continuous Field: F(x,y,z) has continuous C¹ partial derivatives on an open region containing S.
3. Circulation: Line integral along perimeter curve equals surface summation of infinitesimal vortices!
Curl Determinant:∇×F = det [i, j, k; ∂/∂x, ∂/∂y, ∂/∂z; P, Q, R]
Differential 1-Form:∫_{∂S} ω = ∫_S dω (Generalized Stokes)
Closed Boundary:∂(∂S) = ∅ (Boundary of boundary is empty)

Irrotational field (curl F = 0) ⇒ Conservative field (F = ∇f)
Theorem Corollary:If S₁ and S₂ share identical boundary ∂S (capped balloon/tin can), then ∬_{S₁} (∇ × F) · dS = ∬_{S₂} (∇ × F) · dS.
Independence of Surface
Key Computation Strategy: When flux ∬_S (∇×F)·dS is hard over curved S, evaluate line integral ∮_{∂S} F·dr instead (or swap S to flat disk)!
Right-Hand Rule Check: Walk along ∂S with head in normal n̂ direction; region S must lie on your left-hand side.
